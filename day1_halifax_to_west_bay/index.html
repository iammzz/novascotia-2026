    🗺️ Route Map & Overview

    The Marine Drive (Route 7) hugs the Atlantic coast northeast from Halifax — golden-sand beaches, fishing harbours, granite headlands, and historic villages — before rejoining the Trans-Canada at the Canso Causeway and finishing on the shores of Bras d'Or Lake at West Bay. The ocean stays on your right the whole way.

    ⏱️ Detailed Timeline & Activity Breakdown

    09:00 AM – 10:05 AM: Rental Pickup & Marine Drive Start

    • 09:00 AM – 09:20 AM: Pick up the National rental car at Halifax Train Station (Enterprise, 1161 Hollis St), load the bags, and grab coffee.
    • 09:20 AM – 10:05 AM: Depart Halifax east on Route 7 (Marine Drive) through Musquodoboit Harbour — the official gateway to the scenic drive.

    10:05 AM – 10:50 AM: Martinique Beach Provincial Park 🏖️

    • 10:05 AM – 10:50 AM: Martinique Beach — Nova Scotia's longest sand beach (~5 km). Walk the firm sand and scan the surf; it is often near-empty in October.
    • Direct Guide: Martinique Beach Provincial Park

    10:50 AM – 12:00 PM: Tangier — Lobster Roll & Working Harbour 🦞

    • 10:50 AM – 11:20 AM: Drive Route 7 to Tangier (30 mins).
    • 11:20 AM – 12:00 PM: Tangier Lobster Co. — a classic Eastern Shore seafood shack right at the harbour; pick up a fresh lobster roll or chowder while the lobster boats bob alongside.
    • Direct Guide: Tangier Lobster Co.

    12:00 PM – 01:45 PM: Taylor Head Provincial Park — Coastal Lookout 👁️

    • 12:00 PM – 12:45 PM: Continue on Route 7 through Sheet Harbour (quick fuel/coffee option), then turn off via Spry Bay Road to Taylor Head Provincial Park (45 mins total).
    • 12:45 PM – 01:45 PM: Taylor Head coastal walk — the Bolton Head / beach-and-headland loop (~1 h) climbs onto an Atlantic granite headland with sweeping ocean views and sea stacks. This is the signature lookout of the day.
    • Effort: ~3.5 km loop, easy–moderate; sturdy footwear recommended.
    • Direct Guide: Taylor Head Provincial Park

    01:45 PM – 03:30 PM: Sherbrooke Village — Living 1860s Heritage 🏘️

    • 01:45 PM – 02:25 PM: Drive Route 7 past Liscomb to Sherbrooke (40 mins).
    • 02:25 PM – 03:30 PM: Sherbrooke Village — a Nova Scotia Museum living-history village of 60+ buildings, where costumed interpreters demonstrate 1860s gold-rush-era trades. The anchor stop of the Marine Drive.
    • Effort: ~1 h self-guided stroll through the heritage streetscape and working craft shops.
    • Logistics: Admission ~$11/adult; check fall hours (last entry is often ~3:30–4:00 PM).
    • Direct Guide: Sherbrooke Village

    03:30 PM – 05:15 PM: Guysborough Harbour & the Canso Causeway Crossing 🌊

    • 03:30 PM – 04:15 PM: Drive Route 7 to historic Guysborough; stop at the Guysborough Harbour overlook for a last mainland coastal view.
    • 04:15 PM – 05:15 PM: Continue to Port Hastings and cross the Canso Causeway (a 1.3 km rock-fill causeway built in 1955) onto Cape Breton Island. Pull off at the Port Hastings Welcome Centre — the tidal current rushing through the causeway's culverts is a sight in itself, and the centre has free maps.
    • Direct Guide: Cape Breton Tourism

    05:15 PM – 06:00 PM: Bras d'Or Lake Drive to West Bay

    • 05:15 PM – 05:45 PM: Take NS-105 West and turn onto West Bay Road along the southwest arm of Bras d'Or Lake — Canada's largest inland sea and a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.
    • 05:45 PM – 06:00 PM: Check in at The Shoreline, West Bay (108 Camerons Road). Settle in for lakeside golden hour.

    06:30 PM – 10:00 PM: Lakeside Dinner & Optional Celtic Colours Ceilidh 🎻

    • 06:30 PM – 08:30 PM: Dinner near the lake. See the dining options below (Port Hawkesbury and Whycocomagh are the closest full-service towns).
    • 09:00 PM – 10:00 PM: Optional Celtic Colours ceilidh. The nearest festival venues are around Judique, Mabou, and Port Hawkesbury (~35–45 mins); a Baddeck concert is ~1 h 15 m each way, so factor in the late-night drive.

    🎒 Gear & Day Preparation

    • Parks Canada Pass: Pick up your Discovery Pass or day pass at the Port Hastings Visitor Centre — it covers Cape Breton Highlands National Park (Skyline is tomorrow, Sun Oct 11).
    • Layers & Headwear: Coastal wind off the Atlantic at Martinique and Taylor Head is brisk in October (5–10°C) — bring a windproof shell and a warm fleece.
    • Sturdy Footwear: For the Taylor Head coastal walk (and tomorrow's Skyline hike).
    • Fuel: Top up at Sheet Harbour mid-route; the next reliable stations are around the causeway and Whycocomagh.
    • Timing Note: Sherbrooke Village's fall closing (often ~4 PM last entry) sets the pace for the afternoon — aim to arrive by 2:30 PM.
